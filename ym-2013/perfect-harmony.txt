## perfect-harmony-the-co-operative-that-delivers-quality

Sound too cliché? I guarantee you its not! Its about a movement that's trying to get going on this website. This posting is only the <span style="text-decoration: underline;">initial plan on paper</span>.

There are enough managers, teachers, students, professionals and people <span style="text-decoration: underline;">I know of</span> who think along these same lines to think that I stand alone. It happens every now and then that people who are very tired of hogwash, smart enough to understand what's really important - PEOPLE - and individualistic enough to build / support an environment that nurtures this kind of growth.

<strong>We're not resources, were HUMAN<!--more--></strong>

HR as a discipline, propagates a way of thinking and doing things that only serves to systematically dissolve of our individuality, creativity, responsibility and willingness to promptly act. There <span style="text-decoration: underline;">are exceptions</span> I understand but world over, all of us are in one way or another, caught up in industrial and economic machinery.

<strong>Praise for Inspiration</strong>

I'd like to sum this up in <a title="Alan Cooper: Who breathed life to VB which has a unique place in history of usability." href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Cooper" target="_blank">Alan Cooper</a>'s <a title="User Experience Pioneers: Alan Cooper Interview" href="http://www.adlininc.com/uxpioneers/interview_alan_cooper">own words</a>: "We build Wal-Marts and watch our cities die. We build customer relationship management software that alienates our customers. In software the forest of what is really wanted is often hidden by the trees of technical trivia. Software design is not pure engineering, though engineering is one of the legs of the tripod. It's about solving human problems. You have to understand the human side of things."

In paraphrasing a <a title="Amazon: Review by Maj Gen Jerry Holmes, paraphrased by me." href="http://www.amazon.com/review/RKV18O4GF5CKP" target="_blank">review of Bill Creech's novel: 'The five Pillars of Total Quality Management'</a>, I quote: "<span style="color: #333399;">The way Creech got everybody involved by </span><strong><span style="color: #333399;">creating leaders and </span></strong><strong><span style="color: #333399;">giving ownership </span></strong><strong><span style="color: #333399;">at EVERY level</span></strong><span style="color: #333399;"> during his turnaround of Tactical Air Command allowed him to take full advantage of the vast resources and ingenuity in his huge organization. His leadership of this monumental effort caught on like a religious revival.</span>"

You will see this entire revolution unfolding if you have read <a title="Amazon: Editorial Reviews" href="http://www.amazon.com/Every-Man-Tiger-Campaign-Commander/dp/0425172929" target="_blank">Tom Clancy's Every Man a Tiger</a>: "<span style="color: #333399;">... because its object is not simply to dramatize armed conflict but to <strong>relate the life lessons</strong> of his source, jet-pilot-turned-Desert-Storm-air-commander General Chuck Horner</span>". I especially loved the ending where Chuck Horner <span style="text-decoration: underline;">simply moves on</span> and assumes CINCSPACE yet again trying to subdue the "fighter-jocks" ego with his applause for the men and women who drive satellites. Jocks comparable to our more arrogant Leads and PM's of today.

<strong>The Plan</strong>

I still believe in the <a title="Blog: The Good Fight" href="http://cselian.com/blog/reflections/peace/the-good-fight.html" target="_blank">counter-revolution</a>. I see enough examples in the people around me. With the network I hope to have here, we should be <span style="text-decoration: underline;">platform independent</span> and trying to <span style="text-decoration: underline;">build a contact base</span> of quality-conscious and people-minded professionals who just <span style="text-decoration: underline;">want to deliver</span>. Wanting is the important part. By meeting like-minded people in our forums and joining with them, maybe you can get a move ahead. The forum that should exist here would someday have regular meetings, workshops, career counselling cells, a <span style="text-decoration: underline;">newsletter</span> etc.

An informal talent pool, I am hoping that we can attract managers, mentors and sponsors that I / we know and turn this forum into a fertile breeding ground - for ideas, people and companies. Though no ordinary BE (Electrical and Electronics) graduate, I presently work in IT and am trying to get people into open source things and to build and join COE's / Labs / Tech Forums in their own companies. Any place to join <a title="Blog: The Good Fight" href="http://cselian.com/blog/reflections/peace/the-good-fight.html" target="_blank">the good fight</a> - the fight for knowledge, the fight to become better, be recognized for it and to learn to stand strongly on our own to feet and give back to the system(s) that's been supporting us all our lives long.

In simple terms, we strive to align these groups of people in a way that promotes maximum mutual benefit - clients, companies, professionals, their families and society.

<strong>Keeping it alive</strong>
